The Problem

Owning a car costs money whether you drive it or not.

Insurance, financing, and depreciation run on a monthly clock — regardless of how many days the car actually leaves the driveway. Most owners who try renting it out on their own quit within a few months, not because the demand isn't there, but because the operational load is.

Read The Fine Print

Revenue is only one part of the equation.

Gross revenue is not the same as profit, and co-hosting isn't completely passive. A full picture of vehicle economics includes:

Revenue In

Gross trip revenue

Total revenue generated by bookings, before any deductions.

Platform

Turo's host fee

Turo's own cut, deducted before you ever receive a payout — varies by protection plan.

Management

604 Kars fee

Your selected management percentage — 25%, 30%, or 35% of your Turo payout.

Turnover

Cleaning

Turnover and cleaning costs between trips.

Upkeep

Maintenance

Oil changes, tires, brakes, and general wear.

Real Cost

Depreciation

The single largest true cost of rental use — added mileage accelerates resale-value loss.

Set The Right Expectations

Co-hosting isn't completely passive.

  • Vehicles accumulate mileage
  • Maintenance is required
  • Demand fluctuates seasonally
  • Revenue varies month to month
  • Vehicles experience normal wear
  • Bookings aren't guaranteed

Our job is to manage the operational complexity — not to promise guaranteed income.
